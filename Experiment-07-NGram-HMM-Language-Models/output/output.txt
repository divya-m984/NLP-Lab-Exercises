6. CONCEPTUAL COMPARISON: N-GRAM vs HMM
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  N-Gram models are evaluated as word-sequence predictors using
  perplexity and next-word accuracy. They model P(word | context)
  directly from surface-level word co-occurrences.

  The HMM is evaluated as a hidden-state sequence-labeling model using
  POS-tagging accuracy and F1-score. It models the joint probability
  P(words, tags) through emission and transition probabilities.

  Both approaches capture local sequence dependencies:
  - N-Grams use fixed-length word windows (unigram/bigram/trigram).
  - HMMs use latent POS states with Markov transition assumptions.

  Their numerical metrics measure fundamentally different tasks:
  - Perplexity measures how well the model predicts the next word.
  - POS accuracy measures how well the model recovers hidden labels.
  Therefore, a direct numerical comparison between N-Gram perplexity
  and HMM POS accuracy would be misleading and is not performed.

8. RESULT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  N-Gram and Hidden Markov Model language models were successfully
  constructed and evaluated on a balanced corpus of 1000 tweets from
  NLTK twitter_samples. The Unigram model achieved the lowest
  average perplexity among the N-Gram models. The HMM achieved a
